Topic: Waves
Wave properties — key definitions
Wave quantities and units
AmplitudeMaximum displacement from the rest position. Larger amplitude = more energy. Measured in metres (m).
Wavelength (λ)Distance from one point to the next identical point on the wave (e.g. crest to crest). Unit: metres (m).
Frequency (f)Number of complete waves per second. Unit: hertz (Hz). 1 Hz = 1 wave per second.
Period (T)Time for one complete wave. T = 1/f. Unit: seconds (s).
Wave speed (v)Speed at which the wave transfers energy. Unit: m/s.
v = f × λ
T = 1 ÷ f
f = 1 ÷ T
Speed of light / EM waves in air/vacuum: c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s
Speed of sound in air: ~340 m/s
Transverse vs longitudinal waves

Transverse waves

OscillationPerpendicular (at right angles) to the direction of energy transfer
ExamplesAll electromagnetic waves (light, microwaves, X-rays, gamma, UV, IR, radio), water waves, waves on a string
Can travel throughVacuum (EM waves) and matter

Longitudinal waves

OscillationParallel to the direction of energy transfer. Has compressions and rarefactions.
ExamplesSound waves, ultrasound, seismic P-waves
Cannot travel throughA vacuum — requires a medium (particles to compress)

Reflection
Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection
NormalPerpendicular line to the surface at the point of incidence. All angles measured FROM the normal.
Regular reflectionFrom smooth surfaces (mirrors, calm water). Parallel rays remain parallel → clear image.
Diffuse reflectionFrom rough surfaces (paper, walls). Rays scattered in all directions. No clear image. Law of reflection still applies at each point.

Plane mirror image properties

  • Virtual (no light passes through image position)
  • Same size as the object
  • Same distance behind mirror as object is in front
  • Upright (not inverted vertically)
  • Laterally inverted (left–right swapped)
Refraction and Snell's Law
RefractionChange in direction of a wave as it crosses a boundary between two media, due to a change in speed.
Air → Glass (denser)Light slows down → bends TOWARD the normal
Glass → AirLight speeds up → bends AWAY from normal
Along the normalHits boundary at 90° → no bending, but still changes speed
n = sin i ÷ sin r
sin C = 1 ÷ n
nRefractive index (no units)
iAngle of incidence (in air)
rAngle of refraction (in medium)
CCritical angle
⚠ Always measure angles from the NORMAL, not from the surface. Use SINE of angles in Snell's Law — never just divide the angles directly.
Total internal reflection (TIR)
TIR conditions1) Light travelling from denser to less dense medium. 2) Angle of incidence > critical angle. BOTH must be true.
At critical angleRefracted ray travels along the boundary (angle of refraction = 90°)
Above critical angleAll light reflected back — no refracted ray exits. TIR occurs.
Optical fibresLight/IR pulses undergo TIR at glass-cladding boundary. Used for internet, phone data, endoscopes. Very high data transfer rate.
Prisms45° prisms in binoculars and periscopes use TIR to redirect light. Better than mirrors (100% reflection).
DiamondsSmall critical angle (≈24°) → many TIR reflections → sparkle
Sound waves
NatureLongitudinal waves — compressions and rarefactions. Require a medium. Cannot travel through a vacuum.
Speed in air~340 m/s. Faster in liquids and solids (particles closer together → energy transferred more quickly).
Human hearing20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz). Below 20 Hz = infrasound. Above 20 kHz = ultrasound.
Measuring with oscilloscopeConnect microphone to oscilloscope. Period T = time for one complete wave (read from time axis). f = 1/T.
Echo methodSound reflects off a wall. Time for echo to return = 2d/v. Speed = 2 × distance ÷ time.
Doppler effectSource moving toward observer → observer detects higher frequency (waves compressed). Source moving away → lower frequency.
Frequency on stringShorter string → higher frequency. Greater tension → higher frequency.

Gas pressure — kinetic theory explanation
How gas molecules create pressure
How pressure is causedGas molecules move randomly and collide with container walls. Each collision exerts a force on the wall. Pressure = total force per unit area from millions of collisions per second.
Pressure acts equally in all directionsMolecules move randomly in all directions → they collide with all surfaces equally → pressure is the same on all walls of the container.
P = F ÷ APressure (Pa) = Force (N) ÷ Area (m²). 1 Pa = 1 N/m².
P = F ÷ A
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂
Temperature in gas law calculations MUST be in Kelvin. T(K) = T(°C) + 273
Boyle's Law — pressure and volume at constant temperature
Boyle's Law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (constant temperature)
StatementFor a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume.
Particle explanationSmaller volume → same number of molecules in less space → molecules hit walls more frequently → higher pressure.
AssumptionTemperature remains constant. Amount of gas (number of molecules) is fixed.
Volume doublesPressure halves (inverse relationship)
Volume halvesPressure doubles
GraphP vs V: hyperbola. P vs 1/V: straight line through origin.
Units: pressure must be in same units both sides (both Pa, or both kPa). Volume same units both sides.
Pressure Law — pressure and temperature at constant volume
Pressure Law: P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂ (constant volume)
StatementFor a fixed mass of gas at constant volume, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature (in Kelvin).
Particle explanation — heatingHigher temperature → molecules move faster → collide with walls more frequently AND with greater force → pressure increases.
Particle explanation — coolingLower temperature → molecules move more slowly → collide less frequently and with less force → pressure decreases.
GraphP vs T(K): straight line through origin. P vs T(°C): straight line that does NOT pass through origin.
⚠ ALWAYS convert °C to Kelvin (add 273) before substituting into P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂. Using °C gives wrong answers. The graph of P vs T(°C) is linear but NOT proportional — it does not pass through the origin.
Absolute zero and the Kelvin scale
Absolute zero0 K = −273 °C. The theoretical minimum temperature. Particles have minimum kinetic energy. Gas pressure would theoretically be zero.
Finding absolute zero from a graphPlot P vs T(°C). Extrapolate the straight line back to where P = 0. This x-intercept gives absolute zero ≈ −273 °C.
Kelvin scaleStarts at absolute zero. T(K) = T(°C) + 273. One kelvin = one degree Celsius in size. No negative values.
Linear vs proportionalP vs T(°C): linear (straight line) but NOT proportional (does not pass through origin). P vs T(K): linear AND proportional (passes through origin at 0 K).
Open container heatedAir expands and escapes through the opening → number of molecules inside decreases → pressure stays constant (atmospheric).
Combined gas law
P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂

Used when both pressure, volume and temperature all change. Temperature must always be in Kelvin. Good check: if volume is constant, V₁ = V₂ cancels to give P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂. If temperature is constant, T₁ = T₂ cancels to give P₁V₁ = P₂V₂.
